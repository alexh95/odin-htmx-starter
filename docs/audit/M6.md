# M6 audit: Comms (chat, notify, uploads) on accounts and sessions

An independent security audit of milestone M6 ("Comms", PLAN §14.1, §14.2, §17), by an agent that did not write the
code, on 2026-11-04, before the first family invite. It applies the review rubric (§19.2.2), the severity scale
(§19.2.3, calibrated as M4.md and M5.md calibrated it) and D15's crash-zone rules to `features/chat` (rooms, private
rooms and invites, DMs, moderation, reports, blocks, retention, FTS search, images, the four transports, the Auto
connection), `features/notify`, `features/uploads`, the stores in both families, the hub's kick and revoke paths,
`authz.keep_connection`, F-23 and `deploy/compose.yaml`'s diff. Accounts and sessions (audited at M4) were re-read only
against how chat and uploads now use them.

**Method.** Read-only. A performance run shared the machine, so nothing was built, checked, tested or run: no `odin
build/check/test`, no `build.sh`, no server, no drills, no k6, no Playwright, and no network host was touched. Every
finding comes from reading the source at `21961ce` (`21961ce642306d52c9eb981a86430fc406460ba4`, HEAD when the read began
and when it ended); line numbers are at that commit. Another session changed the working tree during the read
(`features/notify`, `stores/*/notify`, `docs/PLAN.md`, `docs/design/DECISIONS.md`, `docs/perf/CHAT-BUDGETS.md`, `TODO.md`,
`app.css`, one spec, and an untracked perf CSV): those were read and cited through `git show 21961ce:<path>`, not from
the disk. Where a finding rests on the toolchain, `core:image/jpeg`, `core:image/png`, `core:compress`,
`core:mem/virtual` and `core:nbio` were read at the installed Odin (dev-2026-10, `84bc3fc`, the build M4.md names as the
pinned one). Numbers of bytes are arithmetic from the code, not measurements. What is inferred rather than read says so,
and names the test that would settle it. No exploit file was built or tried.

**Result (at `21961ce`).** 0 CRITICAL, 2 HIGH, 5 MEDIUM, 8 LOW, 1 NIT; "Resolution" says what became of each. Two HIGH findings block the first invite by §19.2.1's rule:
a hostile JPEG of 37 bytes that reaches `core:image` leaks 45 % of Houston's memory per upload and can write outside
its allocation (M6-01), and reports have no limit and no cap, so one member can plant rows that OOM-kill the process each
time a moderator opens `/chat` (M6-02).

**Privacy.** No path was found by which a non-member reads a private room's or a DM's messages, members, search hits,
presence, typing, unread counts or invites, on any transport, nor by which a moderator reads one outside a report's
copy. What follows is mostly about availability: every HIGH and MEDIUM is one logged-in member hurting the service, not
one member reading another's words. "Checked, with no finding" says what that rests on.

### M6-01 · HIGH · a hostile JPEG reaches a decode loop with no bounds checks

**Where.** `uploads.check` (`features/uploads/uploads.odin:361-401`) trusts `read_header` (`:289-332`) to keep from
`core:image` what it can't take: a second SOF, a Huffman table past 176 symbols, a size past `MAX_PIXELS`. Neither it nor
the decoder bounds a frame's sampling factors. In `core:image/jpeg/jpeg.odin` the only check on a component other than Y
is `:636`, `if horizontal_sampling != 1 && vertical_sampling != 1`, so Y at 1×1 and Cb at 1×2 passes, and `block_height`
grows only for Y's own factors (`:628-633`). `blocks` is `block_height * block_width` long (`:712`). The scan, a
`#no_bounds_check for` over `y < mcu_height` (`:720`), then writes `blocks[(y + v) * block_width + (h + x)][c]` (`:735`,
`:791`) for `v` in `0..<2`: on the last row, `v == 1` is a whole row past the end.

**What that does.** `blocks` is the arena's last allocation (`virtual.arena_init_static`, `uploads.odin:380`), committed
to its end (`virtual.odin:138-141`, `extra_size` is the size asked for). On Linux the rest of the reservation is
`PROT_NONE` (`virtual_linux.odin:8`), so the overrun faults. That is the first half, and on a small image (inside the
arena's first 1 MiB of commit) it doesn't even fault: the file decodes "fine", which is why the fuzz (a 32×32 seed,
`uploads_test.odin:111-128`) can't see this. On a large one the fault is a pure crash, `guard.call`'s longjmp, `t.crashed`,
and `finish_done` answers "That image can't be read" (`uploads/web/web.odin:257-259`), as designed. But the longjmp skips
`defer virtual.arena_destroy(&arena)` (`uploads.odin:381`) and `defer delete(data, …)` (`:181`). D15 rule 3 accepts a
leak per recovery on the assumption it is small. Here it is every page the decode touched.

**Scenario.** A member uploads this, 37 bytes, height 6400, width 6250 (exactly 40 MP, so `check` passes it):

```
FF D8
FF C0 00 11 08 19 00 18 6A 03  01 11 00  02 12 00  03 11 00     SOF0: Y 1×1, Cb 1×2, Cr 1×1
FF DA 00 0C 03 01 00 02 00 03 00 00 3F 00                       SOS
FF D9
```

No DHT is needed: an empty table's `get_symbol` returns 0 after 16 bits (`jpeg.odin:147-162`), and past the end of input
the bit reader keeps feeding the marker's bytes without advancing (`:67-108`), so every block decodes, as zero, in about a
second (inferred from CHAT-BUDGETS' decode times). `read_header` walks it to EOI and reports 6250×6400. `blocks` is 625,600 ×
384 bytes = 229 MiB, every page written by the IDCT, and the fault comes on the last row with all of it resident. It stays
resident: 229 MiB leaked, in one of Houston's 512. A second upload (the limit is 20 an hour, kept in memory, so a restart
clears it) brings that to 458 MiB beside the process's own use, and a third at the latest passes the cgroup limit and the
kernel kills the process: every loop, every stream. The member may repeat it after each restart.

**Worse: the write can leave the arena.** With the frame above the overrun is contained: `limit` leaves at least 20 MiB
past `blocks` and a row is at most 3.1 MiB, so it hits `PROT_NONE`. But `read_header` and the decoder don't walk a file
alike. The decoder's JFXX case `.Thumbnail_3_Byte_RGB` (`jpeg.odin:330-333`) skips `x * y * 3` bytes whatever the segment's
length says, while `read_header` skips by the length (`uploads.odin:314-317`). So a file can show `read_header` a small
frame (which sets a small `limit`, `:379`) and hide the real SOF and SOS inside a COM segment that the decoder lands in the
middle of. The second-SOF refusal, `huffman_fits` and `MAX_PIXELS`'s pre-check all see only the decoy. The real frame can
then size `blocks` to end within a page of the reservation's end, and the overrun runs past it into whatever mapping sits
above (Linux places mmaps top-down, so usually an adjacent one), with values the file controls through its coefficients.
The arena cap still stops a `blocks` larger than `limit`. What lies above is layout-dependent and was not determined:
"suspected" for memory corruption, certain for the wild write. The test is a unit case with `.Overflow_Protection`'s guard
page, or ASan on `check`.

Rated HIGH, not CRITICAL, because it needs an account (§19.2.3's CRITICAL is unauthenticated). `streams` shares the code
but its container's root is read-only, so its uploads refuse at `directories()`; `full` is the exposed build.

**Fix.** `read_header` can't be made to agree with a decoder it doesn't run, so fix the decoder: vendor `core:image/jpeg`
(1,100 lines) as a patched copy, and report upstream. In it: refuse a frame without exactly one Y, or with a non-Y factor
above Y's; drop the `#no_bounds_check` at `:720`; add a block-count check before the loop; and skip every APP0 by its
segment length. Separately, make a trap cost nothing: move the arena and `data` into the `Finish` task, so `finish_done`
frees them on its `crashed` path (a leak that D15 calls small should be). Add a `check` case for the file above (refused,
or at least no trap), one for the JFXX decoy, and fuzz seeds big enough to pass the first commit. A stronger option, if
Alex wants one: decode in a child process with `RLIMIT_AS` and a CPU limit, which also ends M6-03 for good.

### M6-02 · HIGH · reports: no limit, no cap, read whole on a moderator's every `/chat`

**Where.** `POST /chat/m/<id>/report` (`chat/web/web.odin:66`, `:970-985`) is `Chat_Read`. It passes no `web.Limit`, and
nothing stops the same member reporting the same message again. Each call writes a row with a copy of the body (up to
2,000 runes, 8 KiB in four-byte text) and a reason of up to 200 runes (`store.odin:593-615`, through `write_now` on the
loop). Nothing ever deletes a row. `SQL_REPORTS` (`store.odin:90`) has no LIMIT; `reports` appends every open row into
the connection's arena (`:617-622`). `open_reports` (`web.odin:1056-1061`) calls it on every moderator's `GET /chat`
(`:149`) to print a count, and `reports_page` (`:988-1000`) calls it too, with `room_by_id` for each row, which itself
reads every room (`chat/chat.odin:434-441`). The memory family has `MAX_REPORTS :: 1000` (`stores/memory/chat/store.odin:327`);
SQLite has nothing.

**Scenario.** A member with a script reports one long message, in a loop on a keep-alive connection. About 60,000 rows
holds 480 MiB of copies, a minute or so of requests (inferred: a `write_now` costs tens of microseconds; nothing throttles
it). From then on the next moderator or admin to open `/chat` reads them all into one arena, and the process is OOM-killed
under `mem_limit: 512m`. It restarts, and the next visit does it again. The page that would let a moderator dismiss them
is the same read, and it renders on top. dbview is read-only. The way out is `sqlite3` on apollo's volume. Meanwhile the
rows are on the database's disk, and backups carry them.

Rated HIGH as a latent remote crash of every loop, and above M2-07's MEDIUM for that reason: M2-07 was a flood that ended
with the flood. This one is stored, is set off by an ordinary page, and survives the restart. The filer needs another
member's long message, which any busy room has.

**Fix.** A `web.Limit` (say 5 a minute per member); one open report per (message, member), by a partial unique index; a
total cap, past which a report is refused or the oldest closed; a `count(*)` for `open_reports` and a keyset page of 50
for `/chat/reports`; a map or one query instead of `room_by_id` per row. A `chat_reports` retention (M6-11) belongs with it.

### M6-03 · MEDIUM · a JPEG with no Y component spins the decode thread for good

**Where.** The same frame walk: `read_header` reads no component ids, and the decoder's SOF accepts any of Y, Cb, Cr for
each (`jpeg.odin:599-612`). With three components all naming Cb, `color_components[.Y]` stays zero, so
`luma_v_sampling_factor` and `luma_h_sampling_factor` are 0 (`:716-717`), and the loops are `for y := 0; y < mcu_height; y
+= 0` and `for x := 0; x < mcu_width; x += 0` (`:720-721`). The body never fails: the bit reader never runs out, and an
empty table gives 0s, as in M6-01.

**Scenario.** Change the SOF above to `02 11 00  02 11 00  02 11 00`. The `decodes` pool has one thread (`web.odin:45`).
It never comes back. That is one of Houston's two CPUs at 100 % until a restart, the finish that started it deferred for
good, the next eight queued behind it, and every finish after them a 503, so nobody's image uploads complete. Nothing sees
it: the watchdog watches loops and the write lock (`guard/watchdog.odin`), not pools, and `/healthz` stays 200. Nothing can
cancel it, and a stop waits for `stop_grace_period`'s SIGKILL if the pool is joined. Rated MEDIUM as a missing timeout
(§19.2.3), since nothing crashes, though it takes one request.

**Fix.** M6-01's frame check closes this trigger. Keep a budget for the next one: a decode has no timeout, and in a thread
can't be given one, so count it: export the running decode's age to the watchdog and exit 70 past, say, 30 s, or use the
child process.

### M6-04 · MEDIUM · what a connection holds isn't bounded by the pools

**Where.** §17.6 and `uploads/web/web.odin:6-8` say a pool's queue is the bound on what's held in memory, 8 × 4 MiB. The
pool bounds only the copy it queues. `front` checks the perm and then reads the body (`platform/app/front.odin:76-81`), up
to `MAX_CHUNK` for `PUT /uploads/<id>`, before the handler runs, into the connection's scanner buffer (`body.odin:161`).
That buffer doubles to 8 MiB (`scanner.odin:248-259`), lives on the connection's allocator, and isn't shrunk after: only
closing frees it (`scanner.odin:107-110`, `server.odin:642`), so a finished PUT's 4 MiB of touched pages stay for the idle
timeout (75 s). A blob's answer goes the same way: `read_done` hands `r.data` to `respond_file` (`web.odin:328`), which
copies it into the response buffer in the connection's arena (`response.odin:45`), held until the peer has it
(`:382`). The `blobs` pool's queue of 32 bounds reads waiting, not answers waiting. No limit applies per member or overall.
`MAX_CONNS` defaults to the fd limit. CHAT-BUDGETS's 246.8 MiB is a decode beside idle sockets; these come on top of it.

**Scenario.** A member opens 120 connections and sends `PUT /uploads/1` with 4 MiB of anything: the perm passes (the id
isn't checked until after the body), the answers are 404, and 480 MiB stays resident for 75 s. Through NPM, whose
`proxy_request_buffering` is off (§17.6), the bodies still arrive; a stalled body holds for `BODY_TIMEOUT_MS` (30 s) just as
well, and needs no answer at all. With a decode running, half that is enough. Blob answers need a peer that reads slowly,
which NPM's default `proxy_buffering` mostly hides (it takes the response to a temp file; inferred, NPM wasn't read),
but 50 concurrent GETs of one 10 MiB image still race Caddy and NPM's rate through a 2 CPU box. Rated MEDIUM by M2-07's
precedent, the same OOM from a deliberate flood that ends with it, and as §19.2.3's "missing cap". If Alex reads an OOM kill
of every loop by one member as HIGH, it is that too: the cost is 120 sockets and a LAN.

**Fix.** A route-level gate in `admit`, before the body: at most N large bodies in flight overall and 2 per member, 503
otherwise (a route flag or a `body_cap` threshold). `scanner_reset` should give back a buffer past some size. Stream a blob
from the heap or the file in 64 KiB writes rather than copying it into the arena, with a cap on bytes in flight, as
`STREAM_QUEUED_TOTAL` does for streams. Fix §17.6 and the web.odin comment to say what's bounded.

### M6-05 · MEDIUM · no byte quota on uploads

**Where.** `uploads.begin` (`uploads.odin:116-122`) caps one image at 10 MiB, and `create` limits starts to 20 an hour.
Nothing counts bytes, per member or overall. `UPLOADS_DIR` is `/data/uploads` (`compose.yaml:138`), on the volume with
`data.db`, and the mirror copies every blob to `BACKUP_DIR`, by default a volume on the same disk.

**Scenario.** Twenty valid 10 MiB images an hour, never posted: 4.8 GiB held at the 24 h sweep's steady state. Posted (5 in
10 s is plenty), each is held for its message's retention: 33 GiB a week in a room, 144 GiB in a DM's 30 days, and for
good behind an open report. Blobs dedupe by content, so the images must differ, which is one line of noise. A full disk
fails every write in the app (`.Failed`), sessions and chat included, and the status page's disk part only warns.

**Fix.** Bytes per member per day (`UPLOADS_BYTES_PER_DAY`) and a total (`UPLOADS_MAX_BYTES`, or a free-disk floor read
through `sys.disk_free`), each a clear 422 from `begin`.

### M6-06 · MEDIUM · an edit passes neither the post's limit nor its rules

**Where.** `edit` (`chat/web/web.odin:486-496`) goes `message_of`, `check_body`, `change`. It takes no `web.limit_hit`,
no slow mode and no `why_not_post`; the store checks only author and window (`store.odin:444`). `message_of` does stop a
kicked member, so that much holds.

**Scenario.** A member muted for a message, or in a room just locked, or on the far side of a DM's block, keeps rewriting
their messages from the last 10 minutes: each edit is published to the room like a post, and the moderator's mute changes
nothing for them. And an edit is the unlimited write path the post's limit was meant to prevent: `POST /chat/m/<id>/edit`
in a loop sends up to 8 KiB of churn through the writer (with an FTS delete and insert), a room-version bump for every
poller, and a hub publish to every subscriber, at whatever rate the client can reach. A queue of 4096 ops gets refused
past, so others' posts start answering 503.

**Fix.** Count edits against `posts`, and call `why_not_post` in `edit` (and `edit_form`). A delete may stay free: it
happens once per message, and removing what you said should work while muted.

### M6-07 · MEDIUM · rooms, DMs, invites, blocks and mutes: unlimited, on the loop, and one fills for good

**Where.** `create_room` (`web.odin:255`), `invite` (`:626`), `start_dm` (`:746`), `block_*` and `unblock` (`:783-821`),
`set_transport` (`:233`, through `keep_connection`), and `/notify/mute` and `/notify/read`
(`notify/web/web.odin:30-31`) all reach `db.write_now` from a handler with no limit. PLAN §14.1 allows this for rooms and
memberships as "rare and small"; `write_now`'s own comment says it is for boot and threads with no loop
(`writer.odin:75-78`). A flood is a flood of `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`s taken on the loop's thread, which waits on `db.wlock`
behind each writer batch, so one client stalls every loop. And `MAX_ROOMS` is 1000 for every kind together
(`store.odin:30`, `:188-195`), any member has `Chat_Create_Room`, and no route deletes a room.

**Scenario.** A member makes 1,000 rooms named `a1`…`a1000` (a request each, a second's work). From then on nobody can
create a room or start a new DM, in any part of the product, and only `sqlite3` undoes it. Every `room_by_id`, which reads
all rooms, now costs 1,000 rows on each `/chat/m/*` request and each stream's topic check. `notify_mutes` takes any key of
the right alphabet, unbounded per member, and `chat_invites` any number of rows per owner.

**Fix.** One `web.Limit` per member shared by these writes; a per-member room cap (say 20) that doesn't count DMs; rooms
deletable by their owner and a moderator; DMs outside `MAX_ROOMS` or with a higher ceiling; a cap on mutes per member.
Move the frequent ones (block, unblock, report, mute) to queued ops. `room_by_id` should be the one-row query it can be.

### M6-08 · LOW · `marking` isn't thread-local

**Where.** `stores/sqlite/notify/store.odin:150-157`: `marking = user`, then `write_now` reads it, and `marking` is a
plain package variable, set outside the lock, reached from `POST /notify/read` on any loop. Every other `write_now` input
in M6 is thread-local (`making`, `sweeping`, `blobbing`, `blocking_` in chat, `t` in uploads, `now_` in notify, `pref` in
accounts). `pruning` beside it is plain too, and is safe only because one job calls it.

**Scenario.** Two members press Mark read at once on two loops: both writes run for the second, whose Inbox is marked
read twice, and the first's isn't. Across members, but only a count, and rare at two loops.

**Fix.** `@(private, thread_local)`, or pass it in `now_`.

### M6-09 · LOW · the bytes checked aren't the bytes kept

**Where.** `conclude` reads the part file (`uploads.odin:179`), checks those bytes for up to a second, then renames the
*file* to the blob (`:190`). `write_chunk` writes that file by path (`:166-173`), and `chunk` admits a PUT on its handler's
snapshot of `received` (`web.odin:170`), so two PUTs at the same offset are both admitted, and the second can sit behind
other chunks in the `writes` queue.

**Scenario.** A member queues a chunk X with an image's bytes and, on another connection at the same offset, Y with
anything, behind a few 4 MiB chunks of a second upload. X lands; they finish; the decode reads X and starts; Y lands in
the part file; the rename keeps Y under sha256(X). The blob now holds unchecked bytes. Its row still says PNG or JPEG, it
goes out as `image/*` with `nosniff`, and nothing server-side decodes a blob again, so this doesn't become script: the
harm is that a blob's address stops naming its content (a later upload of the real X dedupes onto Y, so the victim's post
shows the attacker's picture) and that §17.6's "SVG and HTML never get in" stops being true on disk. A chunk that lands
after the rename recreates `parts/<id>` (`.Create`), an orphan no sweep finds.

**Fix.** Write the blob from `data`, through a temp file and a rename, and remove the part; the file can then change under
nothing. Have `write_chunk` open without `.Create` past the first chunk, or close the row to `Finishing` before the read.

### M6-10 · LOW · a blob's lifetime has check-then-act gaps

**Where and how.** All these are windows of milliseconds, and each costs one image, never a leak of one:
- A post in flight isn't a holder. `check_post` (`chat/chat.odin:497-503`) sees the row, then the post sits in the writer's
  queue. A member's delete (`released`, `:508`) or the sweep (`uploads.odin:155-157`) asks `held`, gets false and erases.
  The message lands naming a blob that's gone: a broken image.
- `sweep` lists blobs older than a day, then erases each with no second look at `at` (`SQL_DROP_BLOB` has no `at` guard), so
  an upload of the same bytes in between gets a sha back and the file goes.
- `conclude` takes `os.exists` for a dedupe and removes its part (`:188-189`) while `erase` has dropped the row and not yet
  the file (`:136-137`): the row comes back with no file. A row with no file answers 404 and goes in the sweep.
- A moderator's purge racing a post leaves a message naming the sha with no blob row. A later upload of the same image
  brings the row back, and that message shows the removed image again. §17.6's "a re-upload is a new post" doesn't hold for it.

`held` itself doesn't fail open: `blob_held` starts true and a failed read leaves it true (`store.odin:670-680`). The memory
family's walks its rings and open reports under the lock, so a message past its 100-message ring there lets its blob go, which
is that family's history anyway. The sweep goes by rows, so a blob with a holder is safe.

**Fix.** Check holders inside the delete's own transaction where they share a database (`DELETE … WHERE at < ? AND NOT
EXISTS (…)`, with chat's holder as SQL it hands in), re-check the blob's row inside `post_run` and clear `blob` if it has
gone, and order `erase` file first. The first is worth doing; the rest can wait.

### M6-11 · LOW · what a removal leaves

**Where.** `announce` stores an 80-rune excerpt of each DM, and each mention, in the recipient's `notify_items`
(`web.odin:424`, `:431`; `views.odin:633-667`). A member's delete and a moderator's removal don't touch it: it stays until
read and 30 days old, or until 200 newer items push it out, so an unread one has no age limit. `chat_reports` keeps the
whole copy for ever (`store.odin:92`: resolving sets a flag; `SQL_UNBLOB_REPORTS` clears only the image), with no retention
and no page that shows closed ones. The copies go to the nightly backups.

**Scenario.** A message removed as abusive, or a DM's sender deleting a photo's caption sent to the wrong person, stays
readable in the other's Inbox; and a reported DM's text outlives the room's 30 days in the database, whose admin can read it
in dbview. §14.1 says retention doubles as data minimisation (07 §1.5), and PLAN's text doesn't carve these out.

**Fix.** Keep the Inbox line to the fact ("X wrote to you") or clear it by `ref` on a delete, and a removal; blank a report's
`body` and `blob` at resolve, or a set time after, keeping the row for the record.

### M6-12 · LOW · three gaps at streams' edges

- **`subscribe` skips M4-09's check.** `/events` reads `revoked_since` under the hub mutex (`events.odin:98`). The socket's
  and multipart's own `hub.subscribe` doesn't (`hub.odin:506-515`). If the kick is posted between `front` loading the viewer
  and `join_loop`, on a loop with no subscriber yet (its inbox gets no message, `hub.odin:338-340`), the stream is admitted
  with the old grant and nothing ends it. A window of microseconds, for a stream `/events` already closed. Fix: take
  `ctx.start` in `subscribe`, and close at once with 1008 if revoked. The same goes for `kick_member`, which records nothing.
- **A kick misses `chat.typing`.** `out_of_room` kicks `chat.room` and `chat.presence` (`web.odin:930-933`). The page's
  `#live` has presence, so a real page goes. A stream opened by hand on `/events?t=chat.typing:<id>` alone stays, and hears
  who is typing in a private room left, or a public one kicked from. A topic key's spelling also dodges it: `chat.presence:05`
  passes `room_readable` (`parse_id`) and `watch_room`, while `wants` compares strings, so a stream so named keeps its
  presence showing after the kick (nothing is delivered on it). Fix: kick the third topic, and refuse a key that isn't the
  id's canonical form.
- **A session's expiry never reaches an open stream.** `load_viewer` enforces idle and absolute expiry per request
  (`accounts.odin:195-216`); nothing does for a stream, and the 20 s heartbeats keep the proxies open. A socket opened on
  day 29 runs, and posts, past day 30, until a deploy. Revokes work, so only a session that merely aged out is affected.
  Fix: give each stream its session's end, and close at it on the heartbeat.

### M6-13 · LOW · search runs on the loop, unlimited

**Where.** `/chat/search` (`web.odin:41`, `:154-162`) has no `web.Limit` (§17.5 has find at 10 a second, and this is find's
sibling), runs on the loop's reader, not the query pool D16 keeps for what scans or ranks, and `SQL_SEARCH`
(`store.odin:76-81`) orders by `m.id` across the whole FTS match, so `e*` likely pays for each hit's two joins before its LIMIT
(inferred: the query plan wasn't read, an `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN` settles it). `q` has a
`maxlength` in the form only, though `MAX_SEARCH_WORDS` bounds the terms and the URL's cap the rest.

Retention bounds it: a real family's database holds thousands of rows, so a search costs milliseconds. It takes a filled
`MAX_ROOMS` (M6-07) and months of posts to reach half a million. Rated LOW for that. **Fix.** A `web.Limit`, a `len(q)` cap,
and the pool when `find` (M8) comes; a hit list can stop early by walking `chat_messages` newest first and asking FTS per
row, or by an FTS rowid-descending `ORDER BY rowid` that needs no sort.

### M6-14 · LOW · two breaks of "an op owns its memory"

- **`op.audit` is the request's.** `chat.remove` (`chat/chat.odin:292-296`) sets `op.audit` from `a.name`, `a.client` and
  `reason`, all in the connection's arena (`web.odin:839-843`, `prompt_reason`), and queues the op; `note` reads them on the
  writer thread (`store.odin:472`). CLAUDE.md's rule is that an op's strings are in its own arena, because it can outlive
  its connection. Today a store write's deferral doesn't watch the socket (`web.defer_response`, `watch = false`), so the
  arena lives until the reply, and nothing reachable in normal running closes the connection first. Latent, then: a change
  that sets `watch` or closes on a crash reads freed memory on the writer, under its lock, where a fault is dirty. Fix: clone
  the three into the op, and add `len(reason) + len(name) + 16` to `changing`'s size.
- **`changing` is sized without the old body.** It reserves `3 * len(body) + 2 * len(msg.name) + 2 * len(msg.blob) + …`
  (`web.odin:515`), and `change_run` clones the row's old name, body and blob into that arena (`store.odin:437`) before the
  new body (`:470`). `mem.Arena` answers no, and `strings.clone`'s error goes unseen. For an old body of a few hundred bytes
  and a short new one, the clone of the new body fails, `op.result.body` is "", and a blank message is published to the room
  and answered until a reload (the row is right). On a delete the same shortage can drop the blob's clone, so `op.released`
  is "" and the image waits for the sweep, not "at once". No memory is touched wrongly. Fix: reserve `len(msg.body)` too,
  which the handler has, and read only `room, author, at, gone, blob` in `change_run`.

### M6-15 · LOW · the mirror trusts an empty table

**Where.** `uploads.mirror` (`uploads.odin:221-255`) deletes each copy with no row. It rightly goes by rows so a lost live
disk can't take the copies with it, but no check says the rows are believable, and `mirror_due` is true at boot
(`web.odin:62-65`, `mirrored == {}`), so it runs within the first 10 minutes.

**Scenario.** A deploy or a restore comes up on an empty or older `data.db` (a wrong `DB_PATH`, a volume not mounted) while
`UPLOADS_DIR` is there. The first mirror deletes every copy the table doesn't name, which is all of them; the live blobs stay
put, but a backup's whole point is the day they don't. It reports success.

**Fix.** Refuse to delete when the table is empty and the directory isn't, or when the deletion would take more than some
share of the copies; move deleted copies aside for a week; skip the first run after a boot.

### M6-16 · NIT · small things

- **No one can remove a member from a private room.** `chat_members` loses a row only by `leave`, moderators don't act there,
  and the owner has no kick; a block doesn't help outside DMs. An ex-partner stays until they go. This is a product gap more
  than a bug, and worth deciding before family use. A multi-use invite also lets a leaver back in for its 7 days.
- A name in a private room can be probed: `create_room` answers "That room exists already" for a slug taken by a room the
  asker can't see (`web.odin:279`). A random suffix on private rooms' slugs ends it.
- A moderator may mute or kick an admin; `ban_member` refuses only themselves (`web.odin:913`).
- `q` with a NUL truncates inside FTS5's quotes (an "unterminated string", inferred from FTS5's reading the query as a C
  string), so the search answers 500, not "nothing found". A word that tokenizes to nothing behaves the same way or finds
  nothing. Neither is injection (`fts_query` doubles every `"`).
- `conclude`'s failure between rename and `add_blob` leaves a file no row names, for good (`uploads.odin:190-194`).
- `report_remove` closes the report before the purge and the removal (`web.odin:1024-1040`): a failure after it leaves the
  message live and the report closed.
- `chunk_done` has no `crashed` case (`web.odin:201-208`): a task that trapped answers as a success. A trap in `write_chunk`
  is almost always under the write lock, so dirty, so this is theoretical.
- A renamed member's open socket posts under the old name until it reconnects (`ws.odin:66`).

## Checked, with no finding

**Access control, private rooms and DMs.** Every room route goes through `room_for` or `message_of`, and every topic through
`room_readable`, each ending at `may_read`, which reads `chat_members` for a private room or DM and `chat_bans` for a kick:
`room_page`, `post_message`, `typing`, `read`, `/since`, `/parts`, `/ws`, `menu`, `edit*`, `delete_message`, `report_message`,
`block_*`, `invite*`, `leave_room`, and `chat.room`, `chat.presence`, `chat.typing` on `/events`, before any replay (the
perm, then `allow`, then the ring or keyset). `rooms_for` and `SQL_ROOMS_FOR` list only one's own; `SQL_SEARCH` filters by
kind and membership and drops gone messages and kicked rooms in SQL; `members` and `invites` render only after those
checks; mentions and unread counts go through `reads`, so a private room's mention of an outsider says nothing to them;
presence is read only after `room_for`. `notify.user:<id>` is `member = true`, keyed by the viewer's id. An invite's secret
has 192 bits, is compared by hash in constant time, and forged, revoked, spent and expired all answer alike, with
`use_invite` re-checking inside its transaction. `/uploads/<id>` compares `u.user`; `/chat/reports*` is `Chat_Moderate`.

**A socket against the form.** `socket_post` re-reads the room and runs the same `room_for`, `why_not_post`, `attachment`,
`check_post`, `limit_hit`, `slow_wait` and `slow_mark` in the same order. It re-checks `Chat_Post` from the snapshot, which a
role change ends through the revoke. Mute, lock, slow mode and block all act on the next frame. `catch_up` answers once a
socket. A blocked DM refuses both ways at `why_not_post`, at typing, and at `start_dm`.

**Kick, mute, revoke.** A kick and a leave reach every transport (`Sub.user` is set for all three) by `kick_member`; `/since`
checks each poll; SSE gets `gone`, whose handler in `core.js` follows only a same-site path. Role change, disable, reset and
sign-out all revoke (`admin.odin:91`, `:155`, `:179`, `members.odin:284`, `routes.odin:409`), by session where that applies.
A demoted moderator's socket closes with the rest, so its role snapshot doesn't outlive the change.

**Moderators and private rooms.** `may_moderate` is false for private rooms and DMs on all of lock, slow, mute, kick and
remove. `/chat/reports` shows the copy, the two names, a room title and no link. An image removal (`purge`) answers a
moderator with a refresh, publishes only to the affected rooms' own subscribers, audits only the sha, and no response says
which rooms held it or how many. **It reveals nothing to them.** The audit log carries no body. Note what the policy
doesn't cover, by design: an admin reads everything in dbview, DMs included, and the family should know that.

**Injection.** Every view value goes through `esc`, or is a number, a constant or the server's own token (`[%w%-_%.]+` by its
route, written only after `invited_to` passed). Bodies carry `hx-ignore data-disable-scripting`, the hit list too. Inbox
lines are escaped by the producer, cut by rune. SQL binds everything; `fts_query` quotes each word, doubling `"`, so a
member's query is no FTS5 syntax. Blobs: `valid_sha` holds to 64 lower-case hex digits at the route, at post and at `erase`,
and uploads' paths come from that or an integer, so nothing traverses; `mirror` removes only names listed in its own
directory. `set_transport` writes only a known setting into its cookie. Filenames never reach the server, and `uploads.js`
writes status with `textContent`. `chat.js` sets a constant cookie.

**CSRF and the guard.** `same_origin` runs for every method but GET, HEAD and OPTIONS, the PUT and the block routes included,
before the body. A cross-site PUT would need a preflight nothing answers. `ws_upgrade` checks `Origin` (`stream/ws.odin:298`).
The cookies are Lax. No GET writes (`room_page`'s `seen` is in memory).

**Hostile PNGs.** `load_from_bytes` with `{}` checks each chunk's CRC, inflates under the arena (the fix for `endless.png`
holds: a failed allocation is an error the inflate takes), and refuses a size differing from `expected_size` before any
defilter, so the unchecked `defilter_less_than_8` has the bytes it wants; its `0..=` loops run one byte over inside the
arena. `w * h` can't overflow, given the check at `uploads.odin:372`. Dimensions come from IHDR, which both parsers read
alike. PNG reaches none of M6-01.

**Use after free and deferred responses.** `Chunk` clones its body; `Finish` and `Reading` hold no request memory (`Upload` has
no strings, the sha is a fixed array, `problem` a constant). Each `done` frees on every path, including `!ran` and a client
that left. `posting` clones everything it holds into the op and sizes it right. `Socket` clones its strings to the heap and
frees them in `on_close`. The `thread_local` inputs are safe as used: `write_now` runs `run` on the caller's thread, no
`run` calls a store proc that sets the same struct, and `note` uses none of them.

**Reads outside a handler.** `tl_in_handler` is false in stream callbacks, continuations and jobs, so `socket_post`'s and
`catch_up`'s reads autocommit and pin no snapshot; the sweep and the mirror read through `write_now` on the writer's
connection, having no reader.

**Holders and the report's blob.** `SQL_BLOB_HELD` covers live messages and open reports; retention needs no hook; `purge`
clears messages and every report (closed ones too), bumps each room's version, and audits inside one transaction. A
report's blob survives an author's delete, and a resolved report stops holding it. FTS's three triggers keep deletes,
tombstones and retention out of the index; a search can't find a removed message. Retention never touches pinned rows, and
`dropped`/`dropped_ver` move first.

**Rate limits that do hold.** Posts share one limiter across form and socket; frames 20 a second per connection; typing;
upload starts, checked before the size and the rows; the concurrency count's stale rows age out after an hour. The limiter's
key cap can't be walked here, as keys are member ids.

**`deploy/compose.yaml`.** `UPLOADS_DIR` on `/data`, `BACKUP_DIR` on its own mount (bind or volume), the TOTP key's
`:+`/`:-` pair (unset, `/dev/null` is bound and `ACCOUNTS_TOTP_KEY_FILE` is empty) are right. No port is added. Its only
security consequence is M6-05's shared disk.

## The member-wide read decision (§17.6)

**It stands.** `/uploads/blob/<sha256>` being `Chat_Read` and not per room is sound for this deployment: nobody can derive
the address of a private photo, it appears only in pages of people who could see the image, and the row and file go with the
message. A per-room check would need a room on the blob, which dedupe and the holder design deliberately don't have. No
finding here. Its real edges, for the record, none of which change the verdict:
- A member kicked from a room, or who left one, keeps any image whose URL they kept, until its holders let go. That is the
  same as the copy their browser has, which §17.6 already concedes.
- A member holding a file can test whether it's on the server (hash it, GET the address). In a family's chat that says little,
  and it needs the file.
- An address is transferable: anyone holding one can post it into another room (`check_post`), which only shares what they
  could already fetch.
- An author's delete isn't the image's end while a report holds it. That is §17.6's design; the author isn't told.

Revisit it when avatars or video share the store, since a holder that outlives the message changes the third point's reach.

## Fork

`git diff v2.4.0 21961ce -- app/vendor/odin-http` touches `server.odin`, its test and `UPSTREAM.md`, one change: F-23's
`_server_tick` (`server.odin:348-352`) now also counts `l.queue`, `busy := queue.len(l.completed) > 0 ||
sync.atomic_load(&l.queue.count) > 0`, passing 0 when busy.

Read line by line, and sound. `l.queue` is nbio's MPSC (`nbio/mpsc.odin`): `count` is an int that producers raise and the
consumer lowers, read atomically here. A producer that lost its race rolls back (`:29-31`), so a count seen above zero is at
most one spurious turn of `tick(0)`: no tick can spin, since the tick drains the queue. The field is in `nbio.odin:31`, common to every
platform. `queued_work_runs_without_waiting`'s sibling

### The removal

**`claimed_size` (`uploads.odin:300-339`) is safe to run on anything.** Every index holds: `i + 4 <= len(data)` guards `data[i + 1]` and
`be16(data[i + 2:])`; `n < 2 || i + 2 + n > len(data)` guards `segment`; `len(segment) < 5` guards both reads of it; the scan loop
reads `data[i + 1]` under `i + 1 < len(data)`. Every turn moves `i` on (by 1, 2, or at least 4) and the scan loop only forward, so it
ends, in one pass of at most 10 MiB. `be32` on PNG can't overflow an `int`, and `check` compares each side to `MAX_PIXELS` before it
multiplies. A second SOF is refused (`:330`), bar the zero-width case in M6-R1.

**What could put a frame past the cap, and what's real:**

- **A SOF hidden from the walk and seen by libjpeg: real, M6-R1.** The walk takes any marker it doesn't know for a length segment, and `FF 00` isn't one.
- A SOF hidden after a decoy SOF: not real. libjpeg stops on a second SOF (`JERR_SOF_DUPLICATE`, inferred), so the hidden frame has to be the first it meets.
- A SOF in scan data: not real. The walk's scan skip stops at the same markers a decoder does (`:333`).
- Unknown markers other than `FF 00`: not real. libjpeg stops on them (`JERR_UNKNOWN_MARKER`, inferred), so what the walk skips past one is never decoded. Garbage between segments, which libjpeg would skip, the walk refuses (`:312`). `FF D8` mid-file the walk steps over and libjpeg refuses.
- A second image after EOI: not real. The walk stops at the first EOI (`:321-322`) and so does libjpeg; the bytes are kept and nothing reads them. MPF's extra images aren't decoded by a browser. Safari's decoder is Apple's and wasn't considered beyond this: test on an iPhone if one's in the family.
- DNL (`FF DC`): not real. A height of 0 is refused by `check`, and libjpeg ignores DNL.
- PNG, a later IHDR: not real. A second IHDR is an error to libpng (inferred), and `:307` holds IHDR first. An APNG's frames must fit the canvas (inferred), so each is within the cap; many frames at the cap are a browser's CPU, the same order as one huge still. Not a bypass.
- A progressive JPEG of thousands of scans: a browser's own limit (Chromium stops at 100 scans, inferred), and §17.6 accepts progressive.

**Serving a file nobody parsed past its header.** Nothing unsafe found. It can't reach a script or style context: `nosniff` is on every response (`platform/app/front.odin:46`), and it makes both refuse a type that isn't theirs, so that, not `script-src`, is what stops a polyglot. Navigating to one shows an image, or text (M6-R5), never HTML, because `sniff` admits two magics and SVG has neither. But the brief's premise is wrong: **the type doesn't come from the row**, and for a JPEG it isn't an image type at all. That's M6-R5, a bug and not a hole.

### M6-04: the count and the buffer

**`large_begin` and `large_end` balance on every path, read through the scanner.** Once `large_begin` passes, `read_body`'s callback is the only way out, and it runs once: complete; a peer gone (`recv` 0 or an error, `vendor/odin-http/scanner.odin:313-333`, `_err` set, the deferred `scanner_scan` calling back through `:231-235`); the body timeout (`_sweep_timeouts`, `server.odin:719-726`, `scanner_timed_out`, for `.Active` with a read in flight, which a waiting body always has); 413 and bad lengths, synchronously (`body.odin:150-153`, `:145-148`); every chunked error path (`body.odin:218-221`, `:264-267`, `:277-280`, `:301-304`, `count_framing`). `front` ends the count first thing in the callback and clears `c.large` (`front.odin:91-94`), so nothing ends it twice. The one path with no callback is `connection_crashed` (`server.odin:760`, `scanner_cancel`), reached only by a trap in the scanner or the chunked decoder between begin and callback. None was found, and F-14 and F-16 removed the ones that were. It would cost one place of 8, for good. Shutdown doesn't matter.

**No Content-Length.** `declares_large` counts a body that won't say. A `Content-Length` beside `Transfer-Encoding: chunked` can't lie to it: `headers_validate` deletes it before `front` runs (`vendor/odin-http/request.odin:49`).

**The 503.** `connection: close` makes `response_must_close` true (`response.odin:433`), so nothing reads the unread body as a next request. `uploads.js` retries a 503 and a dropped connection alike (`:30-46`).

**F-25's shrink is safe.** `scanner_reset` runs only in `clean_request_loop`, after the response went, with no read in flight (`_deferred_end` ran at `response.odin:302`). It moves pipelined bytes to the front first and shrinks only when `s.end <= SHRINK_BUF_SIZE`, so none are cut. Nothing holds a slice of the buffer by then: `remove_range` already moved it at this same point before F-25, `chunk` clones its body in the handler (`uploads/web/web.odin:184`), and a deferred response isn't sent until its continuation is done. A stream's begin handles its own (`stream.odin:145`).

**`_after_sent` runs once, except on a crash.** `conn.loop.res = {}` (`response.odin:394`) clears it, so a keep-alive's next request can't fire it again. A failed send still reaches `clean_request_loop` (`:369-374`), a send cut by `send_timeout` included. `read_done`'s `res == nil` path gives back at once, and `after_sent` isn't set there. So **`answering` can't go negative**. It can leak: `connection_crashed` closes without `clean_request_loop` (`server.odin:771-782`), so a trap in `read_done` after `web.after_sent` keeps those bytes for good. No such trap was found. M6-R8.

### M6-R1 · MEDIUM · a frame hidden from the walk by `FF 00`

**Where.** After its no-length cases, `claimed_size` treats every other marker byte as a segment with a length (`uploads.odin:324-327`), `0x00` included. libjpeg doesn't: `next_marker` reads `FF 00` as stuffing, discards it, and goes on scanning **byte by byte** for the next `FF` (inferred, from `jdmarker.c`; libjpeg-turbo, and so Chrome and Firefox, share it). The two parsers part there, and only there: see "The removal".

**Scenario.** A member uploads:

```
FF D8
FF 00  LL LL                                 the walk skips LLLL bytes; libjpeg skips only these four
  FF C0 00 11 08 FF DC FF DC 03 ...          a real frame, 65500×65500, with its DQT, DHT, SOS and scan
FF C0 00 0B 08 00 01 00 01 01 01 11 00       a 1×1 decoy, which is what `check` reports
FF DA ... FF D9
```

`check` passes it as 1×1, under 10 MiB, and every member who opens the room asks their browser for a 4.3-gigapixel decode, stored, on every load of that page, up to whatever the browser's limits do. The moderator's `/chat/reports` shows the same `<img>` (`chat/views/views.odin:573`). §17.6 stakes exactly this on the walk: "a decoy can't get past the cap in a browser". What a browser does with it wasn't tried: Chromium may fail the allocation, or scale it on a phone. Rated MEDIUM as a missing cap; it touches no data and no server. `fuzz_the_header_walk` can't see it: it holds `check` to the size `check` itself read, and has no browser's parse to compare with (`uploads_test.odin:120`).

Related, and not reachable with libjpeg: a first SOF of width 0 passes `width != 0` (`:330`), so a "second" SOF is accepted. libjpeg refuses the empty frame (inferred).

**Fix.** Refuse what isn't a defined marker with a length: `0x00`, `0x02..=0xBF`, `0xC8`, `0xF0..=0xFD` return `0, 0, false` before `be16`. Track a `seen_sof` flag rather than `width != 0`. Add both to `uploads_test.odin`, and open the file above in Chrome before and after, which settles the inference.

### M6-R2 · MEDIUM · a body under the threshold isn't counted

**Where.** `declares_large` counts from `LARGE_BODY`, 1 MiB (`limit.odin:67`, `:79-82`), while the only route it serves accepts 4 MiB and every other route stops at 64 KiB. Between 64 KiB and 1 MiB nothing counts. The buffer doubles when full, and `resize` zeroes what it adds (`base/runtime/core_builtin.odin:1472-1475`, `:1493`), so it's resident.

**Scenario.** As M6-04's, with one number changed. A member opens connections and sends `PUT /uploads/1` with `Content-Length: 1048575`, all but the last byte, and stops. Headers plus body pass the 1 MiB buffer, which doubles: 2 MiB resident a socket, with no perm beyond `Chat_Attach` and no upload row (the body is read before the id is looked at), held for `BODY_TIMEOUT_MS`, 30 s, and then again. About 240 sockets is 480 MiB of Houston's 512. `MAX_CONNS` is the fd limit and nothing else counts them. M6-04's fix moved that from its 60 to 120 sockets to 240. Through NPM with `proxy_request_buffering off` a stall passes through (inferred, as in M6-04).

A second way, for a client that reaches the app directly, since a proxy doesn't pipeline (inferred): a 4 MiB PUT followed in the same stream by 65 KiB of the next request. `scanner_reset` keeps the buffer, because `s.end > SHRINK_BUF_SIZE` (`scanner.odin:114`), while `front` released the count when the body was read (`front.odin:91-94`). The next request, under 1 MiB and stalled, then holds an 8 MiB buffer uncounted for 30 s.

**Fix.** Count everything a large route reads past what any other route may: `LARGE_BODY :: 64 << 10`, or `n > MAX_BODY`. The worst uncounted body is then what a POST could always cost. For the second way, shrink to `max(SHRINK_BUF_SIZE, s.end)` at the reset instead of skipping.

### M6-R3 · MEDIUM · three writes on the loop still have no limit

**Where.** `write_allowed` guards rooms, DMs, invites made, blocks, the setting, Remove and Allow. It isn't on `join_room` (`chat/web/web.odin:753`, to `use_invite`, `stores/sqlite/chat/store.odin:872`), `revoke_invite` (`:688`, to `store.odin:856`) or `leave_room` (`:774`, to `store.odin:267`). Each takes the write lock on a loop's thread even when it changes nothing: `use_invite` opens its transaction before finding the member already in (`store.odin:876`, `:893`), and a revoke of a revoked id does too (`:859`).

**Scenario.** M6-07's, unchanged. Any member can make a private room and so an invite; `POST /chat/join/<their own token>` in a loop on keep-alive is a flood of write locks taken on loops, behind each writer batch, stalling every loop and turning others' posts into 503s. Join and leave alternating on a multi-use invite also moves `uses` for ever. Rated as M6-07's half was: a flood that ends with it. The moderators' routes (`lock`, `slow`, `mute`, `kick`, `dismiss`) are also unlimited; they're trusted, and that's fine.

**Fix.** `write_allowed` first in all three. For `join_room`, also answer a member already in from `chat.reads`, before the transaction.

### M6-R4 · LOW · a join in flight can land after a Remove

**Where.** `join_room` reads `kept_out`, then `accept_invite` writes (`chat/web/web.odin:756-760`). `use_invite` re-checks the invite inside its transaction, not the ban (`stores/sqlite/chat/store.odin:876-899`). `chat.remove_member` is two transactions, the ban and then the leave (`chat/chat.odin:305-309`).

**Scenario.** A member who knows it's coming, holding the multi-use link they came in by, posts to `/chat/join/<token>` in a loop (M6-R3 lets them). A request reads `kept_out` from its snapshot before the ban commits, waits on `db.wlock`, and takes it after the leave. `already` is false, so `add_member` puts them back. They're in `chat_members` with a `.Removed` row, listed under both headings, and `reads` says yes, because it asks only `is_member`. `out_of_room` ran before they got back, so nothing ends what they open next. The owner sees them in the list and can remove them again. Inferred from the lock order, not run; it needs a request in a window of tens of microseconds, at a moment the attacker can't see.

**Fix.** In `use_invite`'s transaction, refuse when `SQL_BANNED` finds kind 2 (it has `room` by then). Do the ban and the leave in one `write_now`, which also ends the state where a failed leave lists someone as removed while they still read.

### M6-R6 · LOW · M6-13's 200-byte cap cuts at a character

### M6-R7 · LOW · the mirror's guards can fail for good

### M6-R8 · LOW · the blob budget is everyone's, and any member can hold it

### M6-R1

**Why only the first SOF matters.** libjpeg refuses a second SOF (`JERR_SOF_DUPLICATE`) and a SOS before any SOF (`JERR_SOS_NO_SOF`), both inferred. So a hidden frame must be the first SOF libjpeg meets, and the question is only whether the two parsers can disagree before it. Everything after the first SOS or the first SOF decides nothing about size.

| Bytes, before the first SOF | The walk (`uploads.odin:311-337`) | libjpeg (inferred) | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| `FF 00`, and `FF FF 00` | refused (`:315`, the fill step lands on it) | discards it, scans on | closed |
| `FF 02`..`FF BF`, `FF C8`, `FF DE`, `FF DF`, `FF F0`..`FF FD` | length segment | `JERR_UNKNOWN_MARKER` or `JERR_SOF_UNSUPPORTED`: stops | what's skipped is never decoded |
| fill, `FF FF xx` | steps one byte (`:317`) | swallows the same | agree |
| bytes between segments | refused (`:312`) | skips them | the safe side |
| segment past the end | refused (`:327`) | runs out of data | the safe side |
| `n < 2` | refused (`:327`) | skips nothing | the safe side |
| APPn, COM, DNL | by length | `skip_variable`, by the same length | agree |
| DQT, DHT, DAC, DRI | by length | parsed, and a length that doesn't fit is `JERR_BAD_LENGTH` | agree, or stops |
| TEM, RSTn | no length | no parameters | agree |
| a second SOI | steps over | `JERR_SOI_DUPLICATE`: stops | no frame |
| SOS before SOF | walks on | `JERR_SOS_NO_SOF`: stops | no frame |
| SOF3, 5-7, 11, 13-15 | taken as the frame | `JERR_SOF_UNSUPPORTED`: stops | broken image, no frame |

**DNL.** A height of 0 is refused by `check` (`:358`), and libjpeg gives `JERR_EMPTY_IMAGE`, inferred. Width 0 too, so `width != 0` at `:332` taking a first zero-width SOF as "no frame yet" can't reach a decoder of this family: M6-S5.

**A scan's skip stopping on a marker it reads as a length** (`:335`, then `:326`) happens: `FF 02` in entropy data ends the skip, and the walk jumps by whatever follows. It can only hide or misread bytes after the first scan, which by the argument above decide nothing. In a valid file only `FF 00` and RSTn occur there. A trailing `FF` is handled: the skip ends at `i + 1 == len`, and the main loop's guard ends the walk.

**A real file refused by the new case:** `FF 00` where a marker belongs isn't produced by an encoder I know of, nor is `FF FF 00` in a scan, which the fill step now brings to `:315`. Inferred. `testdata/` has baseline, progressive and restart files, and none was run here.

**Still inferred:** all of it, for libjpeg. The re-read's check, opening the crafted file in Chrome, wasn't done by anyone, and Safari's decoder isn't libjpeg. If one is in the family, the fix that doesn't lean on any of this is M6-S5's first bullet.

### M6-S1 · MEDIUM · an answer without a read drains 256 KiB, and a guest can ask for it

**Where.** `front` reads a body only on `has_body`: POST, PUT, PATCH, DELETE (`platform/web/front.odin:274-280`). A GET goes straight to its handler (`platform/app/front.odin:77-79`), and a refused write answers before the read (`admit`, `:76`; `same_origin`, `:54`). Then `response_send` finds `_body_ok` unset and drains through the scanner, capped at `Max_Post_Handler_Discard_Bytes`, 256 KiB (`response.odin:324-330`, `server.odin:509`), before it sends the answer. `scan_num_bytes` needs the whole body in the buffer, which doubles to 512 KiB, zeroed and so resident, as M6-R2 reasoned. `declares_large` isn't consulted on these paths. `limit.odin:78-79`'s "a hundred stalled sockets hold what one form does" is true of a read body and not of this one.

**Scenario.** No account. Open connections and send `GET / HTTP/1.1`, `Content-Length: 262144`, then about 262,000 bytes, and stop. The handler runs, the answer waits, the drain holds 512 KiB for `BODY_TIMEOUT_MS`, 30 s, and the socket can be replaced. About 960 sockets is 480 MiB of Houston's 512. `MAX_CONNS` is the fd limit less 64 (`nofile` 65,536), nothing counts per client, and M6-R2's route needed `Chat_Attach`. `proxy_request_buffering off` and `client_max_body_size 16m` (`docs/PLAN.md:5129-5130`) pass the stall through NPM, inferred; apollo's `worker_connections`, if it's still NPM's default 512 (`:5137`), would cap it near 250 sockets, about 125 MiB, and at 4096 wouldn't.

**It predates M6**, and it isn't in this diff. It's raised because M6-R2's closing argument depends on it. The floor under it is real too: a guest's stalled 64 KiB POST to no route costs 128 KiB, so 3,750 sockets reach the same place, and no body cap changes that. Rated as M6-R2 and M2-07 were: a missing cap, public. If an OOM kill of every loop is HIGH, this is that, and with no account.

**Fix.** Don't buffer what's being thrown away: in `response_send`, close instead of draining when the unread body is past a small figure, say `headers_set_close` past 4 KiB, or discard as read with no buffer growth. That leaves the 128 KiB floor, which only a cap on connections per client, or a total on what buffers hold, can bound. If a proxy always fronts it, NPM's own `limit_conn` is the cheaper one.

### M6-S2 · MEDIUM · M6-R2's second way is unchanged

**Where.** The re-read gave two ways and two fixes. The change made the first; `scanner.odin` isn't in the diff. `scanner_reset` shrinks only when `s.end <= SHRINK_BUF_SIZE` (`:114`), and `front` releases the count when the body has been read (`front.odin:91-94`).

**Scenario.** A member reaching the app directly sends, as one stream, `PUT /uploads/1` with 4 MiB, then a second `PUT` with `Content-Length: 65536`, about 1 KiB of headers and 65,535 bytes of body, and stops. The first's body takes the buffer to 8 MiB. The handler 404s, as no row is needed, and the count is gone. At the reset `s.end` is past 64 KiB, so the buffer stays, and the second request, at exactly `MAX_BODY`, isn't counted. 8 MiB, uncounted, for 30 s. 60 connections is 480 MiB, one after the other, so 2 a member is no bar: it costs 240 MiB of uploading in 30 s. The tighter threshold narrowed this and didn't end it, as the stalled request can still clear 64 KiB by its headers; a `GET` with a large `Content-Length` (M6-S1) is another way.

A proxy doesn't pipeline upstream (inferred), so NPM stops this one, and the rating is for a starter that's also run with no proxy.

**Fix.** The re-read's: at the reset, shrink to `max(SHRINK_BUF_SIZE, s.end)` rather than skip. Pipelined bytes stay, the rest of the 8 MiB goes.

**Fix.** Pair the stuffing with a decoy after it, as in M6-R1's scenario: `FF D8`, `FF 00 00 0F`, thirteen bytes of anything, then the 1×1 `FF C0 00 0B 08 00 01 00 01 01 01 11 00`, `FF D9`.
