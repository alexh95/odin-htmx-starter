### M8-01 · MEDIUM · consecutive backups pair a voter with an option

**Status: decided, option 1.** Planning took it and told Alex: the poll and the README say it, backups stay whole. Unlinked protects one copy of the file, not a series of them.

**Where.** An anonymous vote adds one row to `poll_voters` and bumps counters in `poll_tally` (`store.odin:231-245`). Nothing joins them inside one snapshot, which is what Q36 asks of the tables. But the backup job
writes a whole consistent copy with `VACUUM INTO` each night (`backup.odin:6`), and keeps seven (`:34`, `BACKUP_KEEP`), and promote's `pre-<tag>.db` and `manual-…` copies beside them. A poll lives up to a week (`polls.odin:32`), so one
poll usually spans several copies.

**Scenario.** A one-week anonymous poll in a house of five, who vote on different days. Monday's backup has `poll_voters {Ann}` and `poll_tally {Go: 1, No-go: 0}`. Tuesday's has `{Ann, Bob}` and `{Go: 1, No-go: 1}`. Wednesday's `{Ann, Bob, Cy}` and `{Go: 2, No-go: 1}`. With
ordinary use and no timing luck, three `SELECT`s over two files per day name every voter's choice: the set difference of `poll_voters` is the person, and the difference of `poll_tally` the option. More voters in one interval blur it only partly (unanimous intervals still
show). It needs no change to the code, it works after the fact, and it lasts as long as the files do. A multiple-choice vote gives up the same pairing. dbview does the same between two moments, and each of those queries is audited (`dbview` README).

**Who can do this.** Whoever can read `BACKUP_DIR` on the server, and no member. That is the one party Q36 binds: Q20's bot conversations say "whoever runs the server can read", and DECISIONS calls Q36 "a stronger promise than Q20's". The disclosure on the poll names only a future
code change, and the README says the file pairs them "for a short while". So this is the WAL's limit without the WAL's window. Rated MEDIUM, above M6-11 (a removal's copy in backups, LOW), because it defeats on ordinary polls what the design was chosen to give, and nothing says so. Not HIGH: no member, guest or crash is involved, and no
stored row is wrong.

**Fix.** It is Alex's call, so these are options, cheapest first. (1) Say it: a sentence on the poll and in the README ("the server's nightly backups can show how a vote changed the bars"). (2) In the backup's `.part` file, before the rename, zero `poll_tally` of every open anonymous poll. A restore then loses those polls' running counts, and the only anonymous counts any backup holds are closed polls'. It needs
`polls` to name a hook in `store_sqlite`, and the same line in `--backup`. (3) Leave as is and record it as a known limit beside the WAL's.

### M8-02 · MEDIUM · no cap on the wiki

**Status: fixed in `c22b1a2`.** 5,000 pages (a 422 past that), the index lists 500.

**Where.** `saves` is 30 a minute per member (`web.odin:44`), the only bound. `save_new` (`store.odin:108-121`) inserts with no count. Lists have `MAX_ITEMS` under the lock (`stores/sqlite/lists/store.odin:24`, `:204-208`), and the wiki has no such row. Each page stores `md` (≤ 64 KiB), `html`, `plain`, and its FTS postings. `esc` turns a `"` into six
bytes, so 64 KiB of them is about 384 KiB of HTML (arithmetic; a real page is far less).

`index` (`web.odin:74-83`) runs on the loop for every viewer, guests included, with no rate limit, and `SQL_LIST` (`store.odin:28`) has no `LIMIT`: every row is cloned into the request arena and rendered.

**Scenario.** One member's script at the limit: up to about 0.5 MiB × 30 a minute, 21 GiB a day, on the database's own disk and in each of seven nightly backups. A full disk fails every write in the app, as M6-05 said. Or a million tiny public pages, which takes one member 23 days, or five members under five:
`GET /wiki` then takes on the order of seconds on a loop and a response of about 150 MiB in the request's arena, against `mem_limit: 512m` (`compose.yaml:129`). A guest can ask for that, repeatedly, unlimited. The figures per row are inferred; a run with a filled table settles them. It would be seen, the index being full of it, and the rate limit makes it days to weeks: so
MEDIUM, as M6-05 and §19.2.3's "a missing cap", not M6-02's HIGH, which had no limit at all. `/polls` has the small version: `SQL_SUMMARIES` (`polls/store.odin:22-23`) has a `LIMIT 200` but its `ORDER BY` on an expression sorts every poll on the loop at each view, at 10 new polls a minute per member.

**Fix.** `MAX_PAGES` (say 5,000) checked in `save_new` under the lock, as lists do, answering a clear 422. A daily byte budget per member if Alex wants one. `LIMIT` and a next link on `SQL_LIST`. In `SQL_SUMMARIES`, the same cap on polls, or `id DESC` with closed ones marked rather than sorted.

### M8-03 · MEDIUM · a diff has no rate limit, and the pool now carries the writes

**Status: fixed in `c22b1a2`.** 30 a minute per member, 400 lines compared at most.

**Where.** `revision` (`web.odin:251-278`) has no `limit_hit`, unlike every other route of the feature (`saves`, `previews`) and of find, lists and polls. It runs up to three reads of up to 64 KiB of `md` and a table of up to 1001 × 1001 `u16` (`diff.odin:11`, `:43-52`, 2 MiB) on the query pool, which has two threads and a queue of 64 (`pool.odin:13-14`). M8 put every
vote, list change, wiki save, preview and search on that pool.

**Scenario.** A member saves a page twice with 1,000 different lines, then asks for `/wiki/x/history/1` on 70 keep-alive connections. Each diff costs about 10 to 30 ms (a million string compares, inferred), so the two threads are full at under 200 a second, the queue holds at 64, and from there `workers.submit` refuses. Everyone else's vote, tick, save and search gets a 503 `.Busy` for
as long as it goes on; `/find` goes for guests too. Nothing crashes and the loops stay free. MEDIUM as M6-06, a route with no rate limit that one member can use against the rest.

**Fix.** `diffs := web.Limit{n = 30, window = time.Minute}` per member, and `MAX_DIFF_LINES` down to 400, which is 160,000 cells and shows the same thing for a real edit. A second, small pool for writes, or a reserved slot, is worth considering if a write should outrank a scan.

### M8-04 · LOW · what's written doesn't match what the code does

**Status: fixed.** The note, README, migration and store comment take planning's words. The migration was edited in place because it hasn't been released.

The brief calls two things disclosed limits. Neither is on the poll.

- **The poll says less than the code.** `ANONYMOUS_NOTE` (`views.odin:20`) is accurate in all four of its statements, and claims nothing false: votes aren't in a row with a name, a vote can't change, one voter or unanimity shows, a code change could log. Its silence is the issue. It doesn't say the site keeps *who voted*, that an admin's dbview reads `poll_voters` (`/bot`'s note says the like of its
  data), nor that someone watching the bars while one person votes can guess theirs. The second is in the README only (`README.md:16`), and the first nowhere a member or a reader of that README would meet it. PLAN §15.1's text is what the note follows, so this is a gap in the agreed words as well.
- **The README overstates how short the WAL is.** "Until the next checkpoint" (`README.md:17`): see "Known limits". A checkpoint copies pages out and doesn't erase frames.
- **The migration overclaims.** "the voters have no rowid and no time, so their order is their ids'" (`0001_polls.sql:3`). True of every query. In the file, a b-tree page's cell content is allocated in insertion order, so raw bytes keep a page's order of arrival until it is rebuilt (from memory of SQLite's file format). That can't be joined to `poll_tally`, which has no order, so nothing is lost, but the sentence says more than the file does.

**Scenario.** A member reads "aren't stored with names" and takes it that nobody can tell they voted, or that a moment's watching can't tell how. **Fix.** Add one sentence to the note, "The site keeps who has voted, and an admin can see that; anyone watching the bars as you vote may guess yours", with M8-01's if Alex takes option 1. Reword the README and the migration comment (the latter in a new file: it can't be edited once shipped, so it may stay and the README may carry the correction).

### M8-05 · LOW · a member's `/wiki` isn't private by response

**Status: fixed in `c22b1a2`.** A member's `/wiki` is `no-store`.

**Where.** `/wiki` has no `.Private` flag, rightly, since it is a guest's page too. `page` calls `set_private` for a members' page (`web.odin:93`), and `index` (`:74-83`) doesn't for a member, although the rows it lists hold members' page titles. `front` sets `cache-control` only for private, nonce and stream rows (`front.odin:93`, `:108-117`), so the response carries none, and no `Vary: Cookie`.

**Scenario.** On a shared computer a member signs out, and Back, or the browser's HTTP cache on history traversal, shows `/wiki` with the titles of members' pages. A shared cache put in front of the site could hand it to a guest; NPM caches nothing by default (from memory). `sessionStorage` is not exposed: `page_html` refuses a member's snapshot, and `history.spec` holds that. PLAN says a members' page is private by response, and a title is the part
of it that `find` is held to protect.

**Fix.** In `index`: `if web.viewer(req).id != 0 { web.set_private(res) }`, as the page does. The same line is worth having on a member's view of a public page, whose body is the same but whose header is theirs.

### M8-06 · LOW · no reset timer on a pool thread

**Status: fixed in `c22b1a2`.** `workers.after_crash` lets go of the read at once, instead of a timer.

**Where.** D15 rule 3 pulls a reader's reset before each use, and on a 1 s timer, because "a loop that crashed and then got no more reads kept its snapshot for good". `open_reader` arms that timer only on a loop (`sqlite.odin:276`); a pool worker "resets before each use". `workers.worker` catches a pure trap and carries on (`workers.odin:190`).

**Scenario.** A pure trap on a pool thread between two `next_row` calls of a read in `polls.poll`, `lists.list` or a search leaves that statement stepped and unreset. Until that same thread reads again, its snapshot pins the WAL. `take` doesn't choose a thread, so on a quiet house one of the two can sit idle for a while, and every write meanwhile grows the WAL past what a checkpoint can reset. S0.6 measured the rate. No trap was found in those reads, so this is
latent, LOW. M8 is what makes it matter: until now few pool tasks read through `db.read`, and every change of lists and polls now does.

**Fix.** In `worker`'s loop, wait on the condition with a timeout when idle after a recovery and call a hook that pulls the reset; or, smaller, have the worker call the store's `pull_reset` right after `t.crashed = true`, through a proc value the store registers, as `store.on_handler` does.

## Known limits

Recorded as asked, and not designed around. Everything about SQLite here is from memory of its file formats, and none of it was observed.

**The WAL.** A vote's transaction appends the pages it changed to `data.db-wal`: the `poll_voters` page and the `poll_tally` page, next to each other under one commit. Compared with the earlier image of the same pages, those frames name the voter and the option exactly.

- **How long.** Longer than "until the next checkpoint". `wal_autocheckpoint` isn't set (no such pragma in the tree), so a passive checkpoint runs at about 1,000 frames (4 MiB). A checkpoint copies pages to the database and erases nothing; the next write restarts the log from frame 1 and overwrites as it goes. So a frame stays until about 1,000 frames of writes after the checkpoint have passed it, and on a quiet house that's days, not moments (inferred from a few hundred commits a day). `journal_size_limit` (`open.odin`) caps the file at 64 MiB, and a clean shutdown's last close checkpoints and removes it. `checkpoint_truncate` exists (`writer.odin:94`) and isn't called for votes.
- **Who could read it.** Whoever can read the `/data` volume: root on apollo. No route, dbview included (it has no PRAGMA, no ATTACH, and reads through SQLite), and no backup (`VACUUM INTO` writes pages fresh). Below LOW on its own, as the brief says. M8-01 is the same pairing without the window.

**A bar that moves while someone watches.** `.Latest` frames arrive within a moment of the vote, so a watcher who also sees who has just voted can pair them. Disclosed in the README, not on the poll (M8-04). The server keeps no timeline: frames carry no person, and a `.Latest` ring holds the newest.

**dbview's read of `poll_voters`.** An admin, on the LAN, stepped up, each query audited with its whole SQL. It lists who voted, never what. Two reads at two moments are M8-01's delta. Not disclosed on the poll (M8-04).

**What's in memory.** The writer's cached `SQL_BUMP` keeps its last bound option, and `w` its last slice, until the next vote. Only a core dump could read them. compose doesn't enable one (inferred: no `ulimits: core`).

## Checked, with no finding

**A private room's or a DM's text.** `chat.find` and `/chat/search` go through the same `chat_store.search` with `v.id`. `SQL_SEARCH`'s `CASE` matches `chat.reads` branch for branch: kinds 0 and 2 (Public, Announce) read unless a kick is in force (`b.kind = 1`, `b.until > ?3`, the same test as `SQL_BANNED`); kinds 1 and 3 (Private, Dm) need a `chat_members` row. A member who left, or whom the owner removed, has no row. The memory store does the same by `kicked` and `member_at`. `m.gone = 0` filters a tombstone, whose body `SQL_DELETE` also blanks, and the triggers take it out of the index. Age-out deletes the row, and `chat_fts_drop` takes the postings. `snippet()` reads only the matched row. `find`'s chat row has perm `.Chat_Read`, which guests lack, checked in `find.search` before `s.search`, and `/chat/search` is `.Chat_Read` too. A member's `/bot` conversation is in no table of FIND_SOURCES. `lists.find` has `.Lists_Edit`, and lists are every member's by design. The viewer in the task is the request's, copied before the scan starts.

**A query as syntax.** `search_words` drops NUL and splits; `fts_query` wraps each word in `"` and doubles any `"` inside it, byte by byte, and adds `*` outside the quote only. Every word reaches FTS5 as one string, so `NEAR(`, `col:`, `^`, `-`, `AND` are text. Eight words and 200 bytes at most on `/find`; `MAX_SEARCH_QUERY` on chat, both cut on a rune start. All three `SQL_SEARCH`es bind `?1`. A word of punctuation tokenizes to an empty phrase, which is the spec's "takes any text" (not run here).

**Snippets and marks.** `write_marked` escapes every byte it doesn't consume, including a three-byte run with a wrong last byte and a truncated tail; marks are a toggle, so forged ones give a stray highlight and `</mark>` is always balanced. `store.snippet` stays in bounds: `prefix_ci` returns a length within `s`, and each loop advances by at least one byte. `esc` goes over `title` and `href`, and each snippet sits under `hx-ignore data-disable-scripting`.

**The new FTS tables.** `0010_fts_prefix.sql`: `DROP TABLE chat_fts` leaves the triggers on `chat_messages` (they name the table), the migration runs in one transaction under the lock before any writer, and `rebuild` reads the live rows, so a tombstone's empty body is what's indexed. External-content `delete` is given the old values in all three tables' triggers. `lists_fts` has no update trigger, and nothing updates `body`. The wiki's fires on `title, plain`, which both `SQL_SET` and the Manual's upsert set.

**A members' page.** One gate, `wiki.page` → `may_read`, used by `page`, `export`, `edit`, `history` and `revision` in `work`. `Wiki_Edit` rows need a member before that. A guest gets the same `.Not_Found` as a missing page, on `.md` too. `list` filters in SQL. `wiki.find` passes `v.id != 0`, and `SQL_SEARCH` filters on `p.visibility` read live, so a page moved to members leaves a guest's hits at once, title and snippet included; the memory store has no such pages. `set_private` runs before `render_page`, and on the `.md`. `Role` has no account with `id != 0` that isn't a member. A conflict's diff goes only to `Wiki_Edit`. Revisions are `Wiki_Edit`'s, so a page that was members' and went public shows guests only its current text. `s.visibility` defaults to Members.

**Rendering.** `platform/markdown` escapes as it writes. `resolve_link` admits http(s), mailto, `#` and site paths; a member's page gets `doc_prefix` with `safe_name`, or a link is text; `link` is the Manual's alone, and its result goes through `esc`. No member's page has an `image_prefix`, so an image is its alt text and nothing fetches on view. `slugify` is ASCII letters, digits and `-`, so `%s` of a slug in `id=` and `href=` needs no escape, and `.md`'s `filename=` can't break out. The TOC escapes `e.text`. `p.html` is written raw, and only `wiki.save` and the Manual put it there, both from `render`. `md_blocks` and `md_inline` share `MAX_DEPTH`, `render` holds `MAX_INPUT`, and `check` the same before any work. Renders run on the pool, outside the lock, so a slow one holds no writer. M7-07's bound on `link_at` holds, and `plain` and `plain_text` are linear. Title, description and canonical are escaped in the layout, and the body's `esc` over title and `by` is complete in `views.odin`. The route caps are enough: `BODY_CAP` of about 200 KiB, under `LARGE_BODY`.

**`seed_manual`.** An explicit `#load` list, with no walk, so `docs/private` can't reach it; `.dockerignore` and the Dockerfile name the same files; `wiki_test` holds the list. Member pages keep their slug (`WHERE manual = 1`), `SQL_DROP` is `manual = 1` too, `SQL_SET` is `manual = 0`, and `save_over` and `edit` refuse a Manual page. Runs once on the main thread.

**The history cache.** `hx-history-cache` saves only if `htmx.find('[hx-history="false"]')` finds nothing in the document, at `htmx:before:history:update` and on restore (`f()`). The layout writes that on `<main>` unless `page_html`'s `c.history_cache && c.viewer.id == 0 && !c.private && .Private not_in flags`, and `keep_in_history` is called at two places only, both in the wiki and both before rendering. So a member's page always carries the mark. A boost to an error answered bare (`not_found`, a plain 403) drops the mark with everything else, and the snapshot is then that bare text. A restored guest snapshot in a signed-in tab holds what the guest saw; what the member then finds in its palette is cleared by `htmx:history:cache:before:save`, the event's real name, which the extension fires after writing `data-htmx-history-value` and before it clones, and core.js removes that attribute too. Markdown can't write `hx-history-elt` or the mark. So PLAN §12's rule holds: **no member's page reaches `sessionStorage`**. `/find` and `/find/results` are `.Private`.

**`hx-pending`.** It copies the form's fields onto its placeholder as `data-*`, so `data-option="<id>"` sits in the voter's own DOM while the POST is in flight, never in storage. `voted` replaces `#poll-act-<id>`, placeholder included, and `htmx_error` removes it.

**Polls, the promise.** Read path by path:

- *Tables.* `vote` writes `poll_voters` and `poll_tally`; `poll_votes` only behind `if p.anonymous { continue }` (`store.odin:238`). `SQL_UNBUMP` and `SQL_FORGET` run only for `voted && !anonymous`. `polls_anonymous_stays` stops the flag changing, nothing else updates it, and nothing deletes a poll. Both vote tables are `WITHOUT ROWID` with no time.
- *Responses.* `poll` returns before `names_of` and `SQL_MINE` on an anonymous poll (`:95`), so `names` and `mine` stay empty. `act` shows "You voted." and no form; `bars` shows counts. `is_mine` has nothing to find. `.Private` on every GET, and no `POST` answer holds a choice.
- *Logs.* The access line takes the route's pattern, so not even the poll's id, and never the body. No `obs` call in `polls`, and no import of `audit`. `form_values` keeps values in the arena. `step_error` is SQLite's message, which holds no bound value, and no `sqlite3_expanded_sql` or trace exists in the tree.
- *Hub and notices.* `changed` is ids only. `tell` sends the question and the poll's path, to voters, at close, in `who` order: the row says they voted, which they know, and no more.
- *Crashes.* `Crash`, `write_crash_file` and `log_crash` carry kind, message, location, route and request id. A panic message here never formats a value of the request.
- *The form.* `method="post"`; `vote` reads the body alone, so a `?option=` does nothing. An unrouted path is logged without its query.

**Polls, the races.** All under `wlock`, in `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`. Repeated `option=`: at most 10 values reach `polls.vote`, which sorts and `slice.unique`s before the store, so `n` moves by one, and a single-choice poll takes one after that. Another poll's option: `SQL_BUMP` names `poll` and `option`, `db.changes()` (the writer's) is 0, `w.err` is set, and `run` returns false. A vote racing a close: `state` reads `closed` in the transaction, so a vote either lands first and `SQL_VOTERS` in the close's transaction tells that voter, or sees closed and writes nothing. Past `closes_at`, closed whether or not the job has run; `now` is taken just before the lock, so a vote can pass by the time it took to get it. A named vote changing: unbump, forget, re-add in one transaction. Double vote: `has_voted` under the lock answers `.Already`. Rollback: every `return false` reaches `sqlite.exec("ROLLBACK")`, voter row and earlier bumps included, and statements are reset first. A failed `COMMIT` isn't followed by a `ROLLBACK` (`writer.odin:88`, where `commit_batch` does), but past `BUSY`, which a held write lock in WAL can't get, SQLite rolls back itself (from memory of `sqlite3VdbeHalt`). A trap in `run` is dirty, and an uncommitted WAL tail is ignored at recovery (from memory).

**`answer`, `tell`, and lists' `answer`.** Lists: `answer` publishes, as the brief says, so a forced close at shutdown skips it, and pages resync after. Polls: `.Vote`'s `say_changed` is in `answer`, the same. `.Close`'s `tell` is in `work`, on the pool, and `close_due`'s on the job's thread. That differs and it is sound: `publish` runs after the commit and is safe from any thread (it goes through `workers.post`, and `new_msg` clones topic, key and html to the heap, so the scan's arena may go); `is_closing` drops it at shutdown. `send_now` is `write_now` per voter on `now_`, which is `thread_local`, and `put`'s strings land in the scan's arena, valid through `tell`. `html` fits `MAX_HTML`: 200 runes of question at 6 bytes each. So a close tells everyone even if the maker's browser has gone, which is why it's there. It holds a pool thread for one short transaction per voter, between which the lock is free. `tell`'s failure can't undo the close, and `answer` doesn't publish it twice. A crash partway through is pure, outside the lock, and leaves the remaining voters untold (M8-07).

**`scan.odin` and `workers.query`.** `new_scan` allocates on the heap with its own arena, and `scan_free` runs once, by `defer` in `scan_done` or on `start_scan`'s refusal. `defer_response` isn't watching, so a browser that leaves closes nothing: the sweep skips a connection with no `recv_op`, and the arena and the scanner's buffer that `s.q`, `s.md`, `s.options` and `s.viewer` point into stay until `resume`. At shutdown the phase is `.Body`, so past `drain_deadline` the socket is only `net.shutdown` and left to the exit, the arena is not destroyed, and a running `work` reads live memory. A queued task is cancelled, `done` answers 503. `post` to a left loop is refused and the scan dies with the process. A pure crash in `work` is `s.crashed` → 500. Responses in `answer` go into `_buf`, on the connection's allocator, and the head, whose values may be in the scan's arena (`hx-redirect`, `x-impl`), is written inside `finish`'s `respond` synchronously (`has_body` is settled before the handler, so `check_body` doesn't wait), before `scan_free`. `resume` restores `tl_ctx`, so `render_page`, `viewer` and `nav_matching` work in `answer`. **Memory family:** `query` runs `run` and `done` on the handler's own stack, which is what a memory op does: `finish` nils `tl_ctx`, `call` then sees `c.deferred` and logs nothing more. A trap in `run` there goes to the handler's guard, leaks the scan, and leaves `td.deferred` one high, which costs a drain its deadline. `pool_stop` clears `query_pool` after every loop is gone.

**Lists.** `find_item` is keyed by `list` and `id`, `SQL_IS_COLUMN` by `list` and `col`, `SQL_ABOVE` by `col` and `id`, and every statement after them acts on those checked ids: an id from another list is a 404, and an `after` from elsewhere lands at the bottom. A `DropZone` takes any dragged text, even from another tab or site, into a hidden field, and the server parses and checks it. Positions: `(prev + next) / 2` with a gap of at least 2, renumbering once inside the transaction, and `next = 0` means the end; a repeat bottom adds 1024, which can't overflow at 60 a minute. `after` itself, 0, -1 and other negatives all land somewhere valid. `SQL_RENUMBER` is bounded by 500 rows. The cap: `add` counts and inserts under the lock, and `create`'s template items are few; `move` can't cross lists. `changes` is 60 a minute per member on all six writes. Text: `esc` over every field; `act`'s `%s` takes only ints and literals. `assign` needs `SQL_KNOWN`. Behaviours: names in the table, `_` a lone `install`, data through `data-item` alone, `hx-post` the same route as the buttons, and `same_origin` covers it.

**`form_values`.** Reads `ctx().body`, capped by the row, with a local copy of the slice, so `split_by_byte_iterator`'s mutation stays local. `form_decode` is `form`'s own, and `parse_id` follows.

**`tools/init`.** The diff composes `FIND_SOURCES` from the donor's rows for selected features only, so a selection can't take a source for a feature it dropped, `--without` deletes the row, and an empty table is safe (`find.init` and `engine` both look at `len`). A mix of engines is `env.problem` at boot. `polls` and `lists` are `sqlite: required` in the catalog. `committed_profiles_compose_again` holds it, not run here.
